Day 1Arrive — last proper hotel night

Land at JFK or Newark. No agenda. Walk whatever neighborhood you're in — the West Village, Williamsburg, Astoria. Eat at a real restaurant. This is the last night in a proper bed for 36 days and the last night in a city without a plan. Make something of it.

Day 2New York City — do it properly

Brooklyn Bridge on foot at 7am: 2 km, barely anyone there, the whole skyline laid out ahead of you. Coffee in DUMBO with the bridge above. Take the subway to Canal Street and Chinatown — pick up any last gear you're missing. The Strand Bookshop on Broadway (29 km of books, $1 carts outside). Afternoon in the West Village. Stock the van pantry: Trader Joe's on 6th Ave or Whole Foods on 7th.

Eat: Di Fara Pizza, 1424 Ave J, Brooklyn — Tue–Sat only, cash only, arrive when they open at noon. Widely considered the best slice in New York.
Evening: Smalls Jazz Club, 183 W 10th St — $25 cover includes two sets, some of the best small-room jazz in the country, open til 4am.
Day 3Pick up the van — first night

Uber or taxi to the rental depot in Newark (Cruise America or El Monte RV). Budget 2 hours for the walkaround, the paperwork, and loading. The van is larger than you expect and easier to drive than you fear — just allow extra time for turns and reversing. Do a slow loop before you commit to the highway. Drive to Jersey City, park up, open a beer. Manhattan is glowing across the water through the windscreen. This is the view you leave tomorrow.

Camp: Liberty Harbor RV Park, Jersey City · $85/night · Liberty State Park is next door · Manhattan ferry runs from here

Day 4Jersey City → Shenandoah
545 km · 5–6 hrs · NJ Turnpike south, I-66 west into Virginia

Cross into Virginia through Delaware and Maryland. Exit the interstate at Front Royal for the northern entrance to Skyline Drive — the 170-km ridgeline road through Shenandoah National Park at 55 km/h. You don't have to drive the whole thing today. Pull off at Hogback Overlook or Range View Overlook at golden hour. First campfire of the trip.

Camp: Mathews Arm Campground, Shenandoah NP · $30/night · recreation.gov · no hookups, first-come spring through fall
Day 5Blue Ridge Parkway south
~240 km of Parkway at your own pace

The Blue Ridge Parkway begins where Skyline Drive ends at Rockfish Gap — 755 km south to Cherokee, NC. No trucks, no billboards, no commercial traffic. Drive as slowly as you like. Mabry Mill at milepost 176 is the most photographed spot on the Parkway: a working grist mill, a millpond, split-rail fences, mountain behind it. Floyd, VA for lunch — Floyd Country Store on Main Street has live old-time music on Friday nights and Saturday afternoons (free, indoors, all ages, genuinely great).

Eat: Floyd Country Store, Floyd VA — live old-time music Fri nights & Sat afternoons, free
Camp: Roanoke Mountain Campground, Blue Ridge Pkwy · $20/night · recreation.gov
Day 6Deep West Virginia — New River Gorge
~2 hrs off the Parkway into southern West Virginia

Detour west from the Parkway on US-460 into the heart of Appalachian West Virginia. New River Gorge National Park: the gorge is 300 m deep, the New River ironically one of the oldest rivers in North America. The Long Point Trail (5 km round trip) reaches a sandstone overlook above the river and the 1977 New River Gorge Bridge — once the longest single-arch steel bridge in the world. Fayetteville, the small town above the gorge, has good food and cold beer. The Cathedral Café on Kessler Ave for coffee. Then east through the coal hollows, following WV-16 — no interstate, just the road going through coal-camp towns, Methodist churches, and mountain time.

Hike: Long Point Trail, New River Gorge · 5 km · free · gorge overlook · moderately easy
Camp: Babcock State Park, WV · $20/night · between the gorge and Kentucky · grist mill on the premises

Day 7Into Kentucky — Mammoth Cave
~3 hrs from the Parkway into central Kentucky

Cross into Kentucky via US-23 or US-119 through the coal country of far eastern Kentucky — the landscape shifts from green hills to something more closed and vertical. Lexington horse country by late morning: drive the Versailles Road past the paddocks — white four-board fencing, thoroughbreds at morning mist, farm gates that cost more than the van. Mammoth Cave National Park in the afternoon: 645 km of mapped passages, the longest cave system on earth. Book the Historic Tour ($26, 2 hours underground, the size is impossible to grasp).

Book ahead: Mammoth Cave Historic Tour · recreation.gov · sells out days in advance in summer
Camp: Mammoth Cave Campground · $20/night · inside the park
Day 8Kentucky Bourbon Trail — Bardstown
~1 hr east from Mammoth Cave to Bardstown

Bardstown, Kentucky: the Bourbon Capital of the World, and it earns it — more than half the world's bourbon is aged within 80 km of here. The Kentucky Bourbon Trail runs through this county. Maker's Mark Distillery (3350 Burks Spring Rd, Loretto — $15 tour, 9am–5pm, you can dip your own wax seal on a bottle) is the best single tour for people new to the bourbon world: the architecture is beautiful, the tasting is thorough, the history is honest. If you have time, the Willett Distillery on Loretto Rd is smaller, cult-status, also excellent. Back through Bardstown: the Old Talbott Tavern on Court Square has been serving food and drink since 1779 — Abraham Lincoln's father stayed here. Evening south on US-31E toward Tennessee.

Tour: Maker's Mark Distillery, Loretto · $15 · book at makersmark.com · dip your own bottle in red wax ($20 extra)
Eat: Old Talbott Tavern, Bardstown · open since 1779 · the bourbon bread pudding is not optional
Day 9Great Smoky Mountains
~2.5 hrs south from Mammoth Cave

Arrive mid-morning. Go directly to Cades Cove: an 18-km one-way loop through a broad mountain valley with preserved 19th-century homesteads. On Wednesday and Saturday mornings before 10am the road is closed to cars — cyclists and walkers only. Elk, deer, wild turkey everywhere. Clingmans Dome for sunset: the highest point in the park at 2,025 m — a 3 km paved walk to the observation tower, often above the cloud line.

Camp: Cades Cove Campground · $27/night · recreation.gov · book months ahead in summer
Tip: The Cades Cove Loop is 18 km but can take 2–3 hours in summer. Go very early or very late.
Cades Cove timing

Wednesday and Saturday mornings before 10am: no cars. Cyclists and pedestrians only on the 18-km loop. If you can time Day 9 for a Wednesday morning, do it. The elk come right up to the road.

Day 10Smokies → Nashville — Lower Broadway first night
~3 hrs from the Smokies on I-40 west

Arrive early afternoon. Check in, walk to Lower Broadway. The honky-tonks run from about noon straight through to 3am, all live bands, all free entry — just walk in and out. Robert's Western World is the locals' choice: Brazilians on a Tuesday night somehow, always good. Tootsie's Orchid Lounge is genuinely historic — Cash, Kristofferson, Kris Wilkinson all played here when they were nobodies. Walk the whole strip then go dark: Printer's Alley is a block behind Broadway and is where Nashville actually drinks.

Camp: Two Rivers Campground · $45/night · 10 minutes from downtown, the closest RV parking to the action
Day 11The Ryman — and the real Nashville

The Ryman Auditorium: home of the Grand Ole Opry from 1943 to 1974. Every country legend who existed performed on this stage. Daytime self-guided tour is $30 (10am–4pm). Book a show at ryman.com if one falls on your dates — there is no better room in American music. Afternoon: walk Music Row (16th and 17th Ave), the recording studios where half the country catalogue was made. Lunch in East Nashville: Princes Hot Chicken on Adelphia (the original, cash, order medium on the first visit). The Bluebird Cafe at 9pm: call ahead for reservations (not always possible), cash only, songwriter rounds — the format where the writers play and explain their songs. Johnny Cash performed here. It's quiet and it's the best thing in Nashville.

Book: Ryman show at ryman.com — any touring act here sounds better than anywhere else
Eat: Princes Hot Chicken, 123 Adelphia St, East Nashville — the original, since 1945, cash only
Evening: Bluebird Cafe, 4104 Hillsboro Pike — 6pm or 9pm rounds, RSVP at bluebirdcafe.com
Day 12Loveless Cafe → Memphis
~3.5 hrs west on I-40

Loveless Cafe, 8400 Highway 100 — opens 7am, no reservations, the wait is worth it. Country ham, biscuits from scratch, sausage gravy, muscadine preserves. They've been doing this since 1951. Franklin, TN is 15 minutes south of Nashville if you want an hour: antebellum Main Street, the Carter House Civil War site. Then I-40 west. Memphis opens up flat on the right bank of the Mississippi. Arrive mid-afternoon.

Printer's Alley

One block behind Lower Broadway. Smaller venues, older crowd, no cover charges, darker. The actual Nashville. Skull's Rainbow Room has been here since 1948.

Day 13Leave Nashville — enter the Trace
The Natchez Trace Parkway begins at milepost 444 in Franklin, TN — 20 minutes south of Nashville

The Natchez Trace Parkway runs 715 km from Nashville to Natchez, Mississippi — following the ancient path walked by Native Americans, then by Kaintuck boatmen floating goods downriver and returning home on foot. No trucks. No billboards. No commercial businesses on the road. The speed limit is 80 km/h and nobody drives faster than that. It's the opposite of an interstate in every sense. The forest closes over the road for 100 km at a stretch.

Meriwether Lewis death site at milepost 385: the explorer died here in October 1809 under circumstances that have never been fully explained — almost certainly suicide, possibly murder. His grave is here beside the Trace, marked by a broken column. It takes 5 minutes to visit and stays with you.

Camp: Rocky Springs Campground, milepost 54 from the Natchez end (milepost 390 from Nashville end) · free · NPS primitive sites · no hookups · running water · excellent
Note: The Trace has no fuel stops — fill up in Franklin before you enter and top off at any town crossing
Day 14Pharr Mounds → Tupelo → Memphis
~2 hrs on the Trace to Tupelo · ~2 hrs west from Tupelo to Memphis

Pharr Mounds at milepost 286: eight burial mounds dating to 100–200 CE, the largest pre-Columbian site in Mississippi. The mound builders used this exact path — the Trace follows the route they walked. Eight low green humps in a field beside the road. No admission, no fence, no staff. You can walk right up to them. They've been here for 2,000 years and most people drive past without stopping.

Exit the Trace at Tupelo. Elvis Aaron Presley was born here on January 8, 1935 — in a two-room shotgun house his father Vernon built for $180 and couldn't pay back. The family was evicted when Elvis was three. At 13, they moved to Memphis. The birthplace is still standing at 306 Elvis Presley Drive: a small museum, the original house, $7. It is nothing like Graceland. It is the part of the story Graceland doesn't tell. You're driving the same road north to Memphis that the Presley family drove in 1948.

See: Elvis Presley Birthplace & Museum, 306 Elvis Presley Drive, Tupelo · $7 · 9am–5pm Mon–Sat · 1pm–5pm Sun
Drive: US-78 west from Tupelo to Memphis · 160 km · 2 hrs · arrives afternoon
The arc

Elvis was born in that two-room house in Tupelo with no running water. He died in a 23-room mansion in Memphis. You drive the same road his family drove between those two places. The music you'll hear tomorrow at Sun Studio and Graceland started in a house you can stand inside today for seven dollars.

Day 15Arrive Memphis — Beale Street

Memphis Graceland RV Park is literally across Elvis Presley Boulevard from the estate — you can see the gates from the campground. Walk Beale Street at sunset: it runs east–west from the river and has been the center of Memphis blues since the 1860s. BB King's Blues Club has the house band playing from mid-afternoon (cover charge kicks in around 9pm). Central BBQ on Central Avenue is consistently the best in the city — pulled pork, dry-rub ribs, the smoke has been going since 5am.

Camp: Memphis Graceland RV Park · $55/night · full hookups · gates visible from the lot
Eat: Central BBQ, 2249 Central Ave · open from 11am · the baked beans are mandatory
Day 16Graceland — all day

Buy tickets at graceland.com the night before — the Platinum Tour at $95/person. Arrive at opening (9am). The order matters: the mansion first (frozen exactly as it was on August 16, 1977 when he died — shag-carpet ceiling, mirrored walls, the jungle room, the kitchen where the peanut butter bananas were made), then the costume exhibition, then the cars, then the planes. The Lisa Marie is his private Boeing 707 with a conference room and gold-plated bathroom fixtures. The Meditation Garden last: Elvis is buried here next to his parents and his twin Jesse. The grave is smaller than you'd imagine. Vernon's inscription is worth reading slowly. There is almost always someone crying. Allow 5 hours minimum.

Book: graceland.com · Platinum Tour $95 · buy online to skip the ticket line
After: Tom Lee Park on the riverfront — sunset over the Mississippi, barges moving south
Day 17Sun Studio → drive south toward the Delta

Sun Studio, 706 Union Avenue, opens 9am ($17, 45-minute guided tour — the best in the city). You stand on the masking-tape X on the floor where Elvis, Johnny Cash, Jerry Lee Lewis, Carl Perkins, Howlin' Wolf, Roy Orbison, and B.B. King all first recorded. The guide plays each song through the original room monitors while they tell the story. It takes about 10 minutes to fully absorb where you are. The Peabody Hotel duck parade runs at 11am and 5pm in the lobby — free, ridiculous, genuinely worth it. The Rendezvous: 52 S 2nd St, down an alley, dry-rub ribs since 1948, reservations essential, open from 4:30pm Tue–Sat. Then south toward Mississippi.

Book: The Rendezvous · 901-523-2746 · reservations essential · ask for the counter seating
Drive: Take US-61 south toward Clarksdale — the Blues Highway — rather than the interstate
The grave

The Meditation Garden is at the end of the Graceland tour. It's the only part of the estate that consistently makes people quiet. The inscription Vernon Presley wrote for his son is not what you'd expect from a father writing about his child. Read it.

Day 18The Blues Highway → New Orleans
~645 km · 6 hrs · US-61 south through the Delta, then I-55 or I-10 west

Take US-61 south from Memphis rather than the interstate — the Blues Highway, the road that carried an entire musical culture north. Clarksdale, Mississippi in 90 minutes: the crossroads of Highways 61 and 49, where Robert Johnson reportedly sold his soul to the devil for his guitar technique. Today it's a gas station and a traffic light with a small guitar sculpture in the median. The Delta Blues Museum is two blocks away at 1 Blues Alley — $10, 10am–5pm, genuinely excellent, covers the whole story from field hollers to electric Chicago blues. Abe's BBQ at 616 State St has been open since 1924. Continue south through Vicksburg on the Mississippi River bluffs, then I-20/I-10 west into New Orleans. Arrive evening.

Stop: Delta Blues Museum, Clarksdale · $10 · gives context for everything you heard in Memphis
Camp: Jude Travel Park, New Orleans · $60/night · 10 min from French Quarter
Day 19New Orleans — French Quarter to Frenchmen Street

Café Du Monde at 7am: $5 for three beignets and chicory coffee, outdoor tables facing Jackson Square, completely covered in powdered sugar within 30 seconds of arrival. Walk the French Quarter slowly — it's 13 blocks and nothing is closed, even at 8am. Royal Street for the antique shops and the galleries. Central Grocery, 923 Decatur St: the muffuletta was invented here in 1906, $15 to go, eat it on a bench. Afternoon: Garden District on foot — the shotgun houses, the mansions, Commander's Palace for cocktails. Evening: Frenchmen Street, not Bourbon. Three or four live jazz venues in a row — The Spotted Cat, Snug Harbor, Café Negril — most free before 10pm, all good, all open late. Bacchanal Wine Bar at 600 Poland Ave: no cover, live music starting at 7pm, food from the kitchen, backyard fairy lights. A different New Orleans.

Eat: Dooky Chase's Restaurant, 2301 Orleans Ave — the real deal, Creole fine dining since 1941, dress code, reservations
Evening: Frenchmen Street · 10pm onwards · walk in and out of every door, all free · not Bourbon Street
Day 20Swamp, cemetery, oysters

St. Louis Cemetery No. 1 at 9am: guided tour only, required by city law since 2015 ($25, save.org). Above-ground tomb city, stacked three and four high, some vaults dating to 1789. Nicolas Cage bought his own pyramid tomb here in 2010. The tour takes 45 minutes and covers the whole history of the city in miniature. Afternoon: Honey Island Swamp airboat tour from Slidell or Pearl River (~$50, 1.5 hrs, alligators close enough to touch if you were foolish, cypress knees, great blue herons). Back into New Orleans: Acme Oyster House, 724 Iberville St — stand at the bar, order oysters by the dozen ($18), get a beer, watch the shucker work. No cutlery needed.

Book: St. Louis Cemetery No. 1 tour · save.org · 9am or 10am departure · 45 minutes
Swamp tour: Pearl River Eco Tours or Honey Island Swamp Tours · $40–60 · book same day is usually fine
Day 21Magazine Street — the other New Orleans

Magazine Street runs 10 km from the Warehouse District through the Garden District and into Uptown — the longest stretch of independent shops in the American South. Antique dealers, po-boy shops, bookstores, vintage record stores, art galleries occupying shotgun houses. Breakfast at Surrey's Juice Bar, 4807 Magazine St: egg dishes, avocado toast done right, lines out the door by 9am, worth the wait. Walk the Garden District cemetery — Lafayette Cemetery No. 1 is free, on Washington Ave, older than St. Louis No. 1, all shaded by live oaks. The National WWII Museum, 945 Magazine St: one of the best museums in the United States, full day if you let it ($30, opens 9am). Evening back to Frenchmen Street. Stay out as late as you're willing.

Eat: Commanders Palace, 1403 Washington Ave · Garden District · brunch $40–60pp · the bread pudding soufflé is the only dessert
Museum: National WWII Museum · $30 · 945 Magazine St · allow a full day, not an afternoon
Day 22River Road plantations → leave Louisiana
~50 km along LA-18 west of New Orleans, then I-10 west toward Texas

Early morning: Café Du Monde one last time, then across the Crescent City Connection bridge and west on the Great River Road — Louisiana Highway 18, the River Road. The plantation houses line the levee both sides of the Mississippi: Oak Alley Plantation at Vacherie is the one with the double row of 300-year-old live oaks ($30, opens 9am). Laura Plantation is smaller but more honest — the Creole story rather than the antebellum myth, guides are exceptional. Then I-10 west through Cajun country. The Atchafalaya Basin bridge: 29 km over the largest river swamp in the country, dark bayou in every direction, hawks on the guardrails. Texas state line by evening.

See: Oak Alley Plantation, Vacherie · $30 · the allée of live oaks alone is worth the detour
Drive: Atchafalaya Basin on I-10 — pull over at the rest area in the middle and walk to the water's edge

Day 23New Orleans → Space Center → drive south
Leave NOLA by 7am · 565 km to Houston · 5 hrs · Space Center by 1pm · drive south by 5pm

Leave New Orleans at 7am. The I-10 corridor passes through the Atchafalaya Basin — 29 km of elevated highway over dark bayou water, the largest river swamp in the US. Stop in Lake Charles for boudin ($4–6 a link from any gas station deli — spiced pork and rice, genuinely one of the best things you eat on this trip). Houston by noon.

Space Center Houston opens at 9am ($35/person) — arrive for the 1pm slot or buy tickets ahead and go straight there. The order matters: Saturn V building first — 110 m long, lying horizontal, the F-1 engines the size of a small car. Then restored Apollo Mission Control: original 1969 consoles, original carpet, original ashtrays. Average controller age during Apollo 11 was 26. The room is frozen at 1969. Touch a moon rock. Allow 3 hours. Then I-37 south toward Corpus Christi, US-77 to Brownsville, TX-100 east across the Queen Isabella Causeway. South Padre Island by 9pm. The launch pad is 11 km across the water.

Book ahead: spacecenter.org · $35 · Level 9 Tour ($100 extra) goes into active NASA facilities — limited spots, book weeks out
Eat: Ninfa's on Navigation, 2704 Navigation Blvd — the restaurant that invented the fajita in 1973 · grab lunch before the drive south
Camp: Isla Blanca Park RV Area, South Padre Island · $35/night · tonight and the SpaceX days
The contrast is everything

You see the history of human spaceflight in Houston — the rooms where it happened, the equipment that got there. Then you drive 3.5 hours south and watch the next chapter lift off from a beach. Houston is the past. South Texas is the future. See both, in that order, on the same day.

Day 24Arrive South Padre Island

Cross the Queen Isabella Causeway from Port Isabel onto South Padre Island — 3.8 km over the Laguna Madre, the launch pad visible to the northwest across the water. Check in. Walk the beach. The rocket you're here for is 11 km away across flat, shallow bay water. At night you can see the Starbase floodlights on the horizon. Pull up nextspaceflight.com on your phone and track the launch window.

Camp: Isla Blanca Park RV Area · $35/night · Cameron County Parks · right on the beach · launch pad visible across the bay
Dinner: Pier 19 Restaurant, Port Isabel — fresh Gulf shrimp, local, sit outside
Track: nextspaceflight.com/starship · SpaceX webcast · X/Twitter @SpaceX for T-2hr go/no-go
Day 25The launch — from the beach

Isla Blanca Park beach faces northwest, directly toward the launch pad 11 km across Laguna Madre. Take a position on the beach before T-30 minutes. Boat tours from Port Isabel take you into the bay for unobstructed water-level views ($40–60, book the day before through local outfitters). When the countdown reaches zero: all 33 Raptor engines ignite simultaneously, producing 75,000 kN of thrust. The sky goes orange. The sound — a sustained, physical wave — arrives 30 seconds later. You feel it in your chest and your teeth before you hear it as sound. It is the loudest thing built by human hands. Nothing prepares you.

Boat tour: South Padre Adventure Tours or Breakaway Cruises · $40–60 · book the evening before · Laguna Madre water-level view
Position: The beach near the Isla Blanca Park entrance · highest viewing count · bring binoculars and a jacket (early morning wind)
Day 26Launch day 2 or 3 — the island holds you

SpaceX launch windows typically run three days. If the first attempt scrubs — weather, range safety, vehicle readiness — you stay. The island is easy to be patient in. Kayak the back bays (AYS Kayaking or Island Adventure Watersports). Drive Boca Chica Beach south at low tide toward the mouth of the Rio Grande — the beach runs all the way to Mexico with nothing on it. Port Isabel has good cheap shrimp. When the rocket goes, it goes. You will not be ready for it regardless of how ready you think you are.

Planning for 2029

Go to nextspaceflight.com/starship ↗ and build your entire itinerary dates around a confirmed 2029 Starship launch window. SpaceX targets 6–12 Starship missions per year from Starbase. Build Days 23–25 as your flex anchor — everything else adjusts around this.

Day 27South Padre → Big Bend
~5 hrs · US-83 west through Rio Grande City and Laredo, or US-90 via Del Rio

Two possible routes west: US-83 runs straight along the Rio Grande through border towns — Rio Grande City, Laredo, Eagle Pass — flat, hot, desolate in the best way. US-90 via Del Rio is slightly longer but passes Seminole Canyon State Park (prehistoric rock paintings, some 4,000 years old, $7 guided tour). The Chisos Mountains appear from flat nothing, rising 2,375 m out of the Chihuahuan Desert. Enter Big Bend and go directly to the Chisos Basin. The drive into the basin winds through switchbacks into a mountain bowl surrounded on three sides. Camp before dark. The sky opens overhead in a way that isn't possible in lower terrain.

Camp: Chisos Basin Campground · $14/night · recreation.gov · book months ahead · the best campsite in Texas
Detour: Seminole Canyon State Park, near Del Rio · $7 guided tour · 4,000-year-old pictographs · only if taking the US-90 route
Day 28Rio Grande hot springs — Santa Elena Canyon

Rio Grande Village hot springs: natural rock pools built into the river bank, the water exactly body-temperature, Mexico 15 metres across the water. You can call across. Boquillas International Crossing (bring passport): a $5 rowboat takes you officially across the Rio Grande into the tiny Mexican village of Boquillas del Carmen — 300 people, one restaurant, one bar, horses tied outside. Lunch, then back across. Santa Elena Canyon: 30-minute drive, then a 2.7 km walk into the canyon where the Rio Grande cuts a corridor through 460 m limestone walls. The river is dead quiet inside. Go in as far as the first river crossing. Return by 4pm to catch the light on the canyon walls.

Bring: Passport for Boquillas Crossing · rowboat $5 each way · no reservations · crossing not always available — check nps.gov/bibe
Hike: Santa Elena Canyon Trail · 2.7 km round trip · easy · short scramble over rocks at the start · extraordinary inside
Day 29The River Road — into Marfa

FM 170, the River Road: 80 km northwest of Presidio along the Rio Grande gorge. The road is cut directly into canyon walls above the river, rising and falling through switchbacks. It is the most dramatic drive in Texas and one of the great drives in the United States. Pull over constantly — there are no wrong places to stop. Arrive Marfa in the afternoon. Marfa is genuinely strange: a small cattle town in the high desert that became an international contemporary art destination because Donald Judd moved there in 1971 and built the Chinati Foundation — 100 permanent large-scale concrete sculpture installations spread across the high desert floor ($40 guided tours Thu–Mon). The Marfa Lights: a pull-off on US-90 nine km east of town, after dark, peer-reviewed unexplained phenomena seen since 1883. Food Shark food truck on Lincoln St when it's open.

Art: Chinati Foundation, Marfa · chinati.org · $40 guided tours Thu–Mon · book ahead · the Judd concrete works are extraordinary in the desert light
Lights: Marfa Lights Viewing Area · 14 km east on US-90 · best after 10pm · no explanation since 1883
Camp: Marfa overnight — Tumbleweeds RV Park or El Cosmico (tents and trailers, $55+)

Day 31White Sands — sunrise to noon, then north
White Sands is 30 min from Alamogordo on US-70 · Santa Fe is 4 hrs north on US-54 then I-25

White Sands National Park opens at 7am ($25/vehicle — covered by America the Beautiful Pass). Go at sunrise. The dunes are pure white gypsum — the mineral that gives drywall its core. Gypsum doesn't retain heat the way quartz sand does, so the dunes are cool underfoot even in summer. The 16 km Dunes Drive goes into the heart of the field: you can pull off anywhere and walk. The light at 7am turns the dunes pale blue and pink. By 9am they're blinding white. The silence inside the dune field is total.

Rent a plastic sled from the visitor center gift shop ($20 deposit, returned when you bring it back) and slide the dune faces — the gypsum is smooth and fast and the slope angles are perfect. Primitive backcountry camping in the dunes requires a permit from the visitor center ($3/person) and is one of the genuinely extraordinary overnight experiences in the American West, but with the schedule we're moving north after lunch. US-54 north toward Alamogordo, then I-25 north through the Rio Grande valley toward Santa Fe.

Go at sunrise: 7am opening · dunes glow blue-pink · near-empty for the first hour · visitor center opens with the park
Sled: Rent from the gift shop · $20 deposit · plastic saucer · the north-facing dune slopes near the end of Dunes Drive are the best
Drive north: US-54 to I-25 north · Santa Fe in 4 hrs · arrive afternoon

Day 32Santa Fe — the oldest city in the West

Santa Fe was founded in 1610 — a decade before the Pilgrims landed at Plymouth Rock. The Plaza at the center of town has been in continuous use since then. Walk it slowly. The Palace of the Governors on the north side is the longest continuously occupied public building in North America; today Pueblo artisans sell jewelry beneath its portal, as they have every day since 1969. The New Mexico State Capitol is two blocks south — the only round state capitol in the US, called the Roundhouse.

Canyon Road: one kilometre of galleries packed into a former acequia road, open from 10am, free to walk. More serious galleries per block than anywhere in the American Southwest. Georgia O'Keeffe Museum on Johnson St ($20, 10am–5pm) — the painter lived in New Mexico from 1949 until her death in 1986 and the light here is visible in everything she made. The Shed on Garcia St for lunch: green chile stew, posole, sopapillas, since 1953. Everything has green chile on it. This is correct.

Art: Georgia O'Keeffe Museum · 217 Johnson St · $20 · 10am–5pm · closes Tue · the paintings make more sense here than anywhere
Eat: The Shed, 113½ E Palace Ave · since 1953 · green chile stew is the move · closes at 2:30pm · lunch only
Camp: Rancheros de Santa Fe Campground · $55/night · full hookups · 15 min from the Plaza
Day 33Taos → Rio Grande Gorge → Raton Pass tomorrow
Santa Fe → Taos on NM-68 through the Rio Grande Gorge · 1.5 hrs · then north to camp near Raton

Take NM-68 north from Santa Fe through Española and into the Rio Grande Gorge — the road drops into the canyon and runs along the river between 300 m walls for 30 km before climbing back out at Taos. The Rio Grande Gorge Bridge at the top: a 213 m suspension bridge spanning a gorge 190 m deep, cut straight through the plateau. Walk across it. The canyon is narrow and vertical and drops away under your feet.

Taos Pueblo: the oldest continuously inhabited community in North America, occupied since approximately 1000 CE ($25 guided tours, 8am–4:30pm, closed certain ceremonial dates). The multi-story adobe complex has been lived in without electricity or running water — by choice, by families who have lived there for a thousand years. Photography is permitted under specific rules explained at the entrance. Kit Carson Home and Museum ($8) covers the mountain man and guide who lived in Taos from 1826. Taos downtown for lunch. Then north on US-64 through the high sage plateau toward Raton.

Book: Taos Pueblo · taospueblo.com · confirm it's open (closed for 10 weeks in winter and certain ceremonial dates)
Drive: US-64 from Taos northeast to Raton · 1.5 hrs · high desert, pinyon-juniper · camp near Raton tonight
Camp: Sugarite Canyon State Park near Raton · $14/night · volcanic canyon · cross Raton Pass into Colorado tomorrow morning
The light

Georgia O'Keeffe moved to New Mexico at 62 and spent the last 36 years of her life painting the desert from a house in Abiquiú. She wrote: "I have wanted to paint the desert and I haven't known how. I always think I cannot stay with it long enough." The light here is different from anywhere else in the country. You'll see why she stayed.

Day 34Cross Raton Pass — into Colorado
Raton Pass summit is 15 min north of Raton on I-25 · Colorado Springs 2.5 hrs beyond

The pass is 15 minutes north of camp. Stop at the summit pull-off at 2,388 m — the original mountain crossing of the Santa Fe Trail, used by wagon trains from 1821 to 1880. The Sangre de Cristo range runs south into New Mexico behind you; Colorado's high plains open to the north. Everything on one side is desert. Everything on the other is mountain. You've been in the desert since Texas. This is the moment the landscape changes. The town of Raton below: Pappa's Sweetshop on Cook Avenue for pie (since 1952). Then north on I-25 as the Rockies build on the left and the plains stretch right.

Stop: Raton Pass summit pull-off · cross in the morning · Sangre de Cristos catch the light behind you

Day 35Colorado Springs — Garden of the Gods
~210 km north from Raton on I-25

Pueblo, Colorado in an hour: the Arkansas River cuts through here and the Rockies begin to the west. Colorado Springs by midday. Garden of the Gods is free, open always, and the most photogenic place in Colorado: enormous red and orange sandstone formations rising 90 m against the white summit of Pikes Peak. The Gateway Rock trail takes 45 minutes and covers the best formations. Day 36Rocky Mountain National Park — Trail Ridge Road
~120 km from Colorado Springs to Estes Park

Enter Rocky Mountain National Park through the Beaver Meadows entrance. Drive Trail Ridge Road — the highest continuous paved highway in the United States at 3,713 m, 77 km crossing the park east to west. The treeline ends around 3,350 m and everything above is tundra: short grasses, marmots, pikas, elk grazing at the pulloffs. Fall River Pass at 3,595 m has a visitor center and 360-degree views of the Continental Divide. On the western descent: Never Summer Mountains to the north, the Colorado River headwaters below. Camp in the valley. At night the elk come into the campground.

Day 38Arches NP → Monument Valley at dusk
~2.5 hrs from Moab to Monument Valley via US-191 south

Arches National Park opens at 6am — arrive then. Timed entry required April–October ($35/vehicle, reserve at recreation.gov far in advance). Day 40Grand Canyon South Rim
~2.5 hrs from Page on US-89 south

Arrive before 9am. Park at Mather Point and walk to the rim. No photograph prepares you — the canyon is 16 km wide, 1,830 m deep, and 445 km long, and none of those numbers means anything until you're standing at the edge. Give it 20 minutes without a phone. Then: Bright Angel Trail descends from the South Rim Trailhead into the canyon — go as far as the 2.5 km Resthouse (930 m elevation loss) and turn back before noon. It is twice as difficult climbing out as descending. Mather Point is outstanding at 5:30am the next morning, before any buses arrive.

Camp: Mather Campground, inside the park · $18/night · recreation.gov · elk walk through the sites at dusk · book 6 months ahead
Hike: Bright Angel Trail — turn around at 2.5 km Resthouse or 5 km Resthouse maximum · going further requires overnight permits
Tip: Yavapai Point at sunrise is less crowded than Mather and equally good

Completely possible. RV companies need vehicles moved between coasts. Expect a one-way positioning fee of $350–500 on top of the daily rate. Book 3–6 months ahead for summer dates.

your copilot.
